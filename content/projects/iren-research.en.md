## Overview
This case study examines how IREN is repositioning power and data-centre assets toward AI / HPC, separating strategic narrative, committed capital and delivered operating outcomes.

## Core question
Do existing power and campus assets create a durable advantage? Can GPU deployment, financing, customer contracts and utilisation convert into revenue on schedule while containing dilution and execution risk?

## Analytical framework
- Quality, location and delivery timing of power and data-centre capacity
- GPU procurement, deployment and customer-acceptance cadence
- Capex, financing structure and potential equity dilution
- Contract duration, customer concentration, utilisation and revenue visibility

## Key observation
Infrastructure assets create an option to enter AI markets, but not automatic demand or returns. The real validation lies in conversion efficiency across contracts, deployment, acceptance, utilisation and cash collection.

## Risks
Key risks include construction delays, GPU obsolescence, customer concentration, financing cost, weak utilisation and valuations that capitalise future capacity too early.

## Research method
The work prioritises company filings, earnings materials and calls to build an evidence chain from claim to milestone to outcome, clearly separating forecasts from realised data.

## Limitations
This is an industry and execution case study, not investment advice. Public sources cannot fully reveal contract terms, customer quality or future financing choices.

## Next steps
Map new disclosures to a milestone tracker, update gaps between capacity, deployment, contracts and revenue, and compare execution with AI cloud and data-centre peers.
