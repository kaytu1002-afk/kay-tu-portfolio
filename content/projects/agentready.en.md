## Overview
AgentReady asks a practical question: when autonomous agents search, compare, purchase and manage after-sales tasks for users, can a merchant actually be understood and transacted with? The first MVP will assess real websites and produce an AgentReady Score with a diagnostic report.

## Problem
Most digital experiences are still designed primarily for human browsers. Product data, inventory, identity, checkout and after-sales policies may exist without being structured, stable or verifiable by an agent.

## Why it matters
The constraint on agentic commerce is not only model capability. It is also whether businesses expose usable interfaces. A readiness framework turns a vague idea of being “AI-friendly” into a measurable operational problem.

## Approach
- Map the end-to-end task journey from discovery to after-sales
- Assess structured information, APIs, inventory and pricing access
- Test authentication, checkout, cancellation, refund and support flows
- Separate machine-readable, machine-actionable and machine-verifiable states

## System
The score covers discoverability, product information, inventory, pricing, APIs, checkout, authentication, cancellation, refunds, support, agent-readable policies and verifiable reviews. Each dimension records evidence, failure points and recommended actions.

## What I learned
Agent readiness is not achieved by adding a chatbot. Core business states need to be reliably read, acted on and verified. The scoring model must balance technical ambition with implementation cost for merchants.

## Limitations
Purchase journeys vary significantly by vertical, and public-site testing cannot reveal every back-end capability. The first version will state its evidence boundaries and avoid treating unknown states as failures.

## Next steps
Complete the scoring rubric, select an initial merchant sample, build a semi-automated audit flow and test whether the report creates concrete improvement actions.
